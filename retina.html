CSC8628 · Image processing · Newcastle

Retinal vessels.

A classical, non-learning segmentation of blood vessels in 15 healthy high-resolution fundus images. One fixed set of parameters. Tuned on five images and applied unchanged to the other ten.

0.67vessel IoU
0.81mIoU
0.80Dice
0.67held-out IoU

The problem

Vessel contrast is low, illumination is uneven, and the optic disc and macula create edges that look like vessels. An empty prediction already scores about 0.90 accuracy, because vessels cover only about 9% of each image, so overlap (IoU) was the measure that decided each step.

What changed the result

Background normalisation did most of the work. Vessel IoU moved from 0.14 on the raw green channel to 0.54 once slow illumination was removed. CLAHE, a Frangi filter, and a top-hat fused with it (0.7 Frangi, 0.3 top-hat) then recovered thinner vessels. The held-out images scored 0.67, a little above the five images used for tuning (0.66).

  1. Green channel0.14
  2. Illumination removed0.54
  3. Frangi0.65
  4. Final pipeline0.67

What it still misses

Wide vessels are found (sensitivity 0.90). Thin vessels, under 5 pixels, drop to 0.41. The weakest image is also the lowest contrast. Only healthy images were used.
